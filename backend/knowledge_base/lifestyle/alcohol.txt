# Topic: Alcohol

ID: LIFE004

Category: Lifestyle

---

## Overview

Alcohol affects the brain, liver, heart, and many other organs. Excessive alcohol use increases the risk of injuries, chronic diseases, and mental health problems.

Some people should not drink alcohol at all, including children, pregnant women, and people taking certain medicines.

---

## Health Risks

Heavy alcohol use increases the risk of:

- Liver disease
- High blood pressure
- Heart disease
- Stroke
- Certain cancers
- Depression
- Road traffic accidents
- Injuries

---

## Signs of Harmful Alcohol Use

Possible signs include:

- Drinking more than intended
- Difficulty stopping
- Missing work or school
- Relationship problems
- Withdrawal symptoms

---

## Healthy Advice

To reduce health risks:

- Avoid binge drinking.
- Never drink and drive.
- Do not mix alcohol with certain medicines.
- Seek help if alcohol affects daily life.

---

## When to Seek Medical Advice

Consult a healthcare professional if:

- You cannot control drinking.
- You experience withdrawal symptoms.
- Alcohol affects work, relationships, or health.

---

## Emergency Signs

Call emergency medical services immediately if a person:

- Becomes unconscious.
- Has difficulty breathing.
- Has repeated vomiting while unconscious.
- Has seizures after drinking.

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is occasional alcohol use completely safe?

Alcohol carries health risks. The safest option for health is not drinking alcohol.

### Should pregnant women drink alcohol?

No. Alcohol should be avoided during pregnancy.
