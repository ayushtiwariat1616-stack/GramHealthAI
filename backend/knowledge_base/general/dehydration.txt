# Topic: Dehydration

ID: GEN006

Category: General Symptoms

---

## Overview

Dehydration occurs when the body loses more fluids than it takes in. It can result from diarrhea, vomiting, fever, excessive sweating, or inadequate fluid intake.

Children, older adults, and people with chronic illnesses are at higher risk.

---

## Common Causes

- Diarrhea
- Vomiting
- Fever
- Excessive sweating
- Not drinking enough water

---

## Symptoms

Mild dehydration:

- Thirst
- Dry mouth
- Dark yellow urine
- Tiredness

Severe dehydration:

- Very little urine
- Dizziness
- Rapid heartbeat
- Confusion
- Sunken eyes
- Loss of consciousness

---

## Treatment

For mild dehydration:

- Drink clean water.
- Use Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS).
- Drink fluids frequently in small amounts.
- Continue eating if possible.

---

## Prevention

- Drink enough water every day.
- Increase fluids during illness.
- Replace fluids after heavy sweating.
- Use ORS during diarrhea or vomiting when advised.

---

## Do NOT

Do NOT:

- Ignore signs of severe dehydration.
- Wait until severe thirst develops.
- Replace fluids only with sugary drinks.

---

## When to Visit a Doctor

Consult a healthcare professional if:

- Symptoms do not improve.
- Vomiting prevents drinking.
- Diarrhea continues.
- The person becomes very weak.

---

## Emergency Signs

Call emergency medical services immediately if:

- Loss of consciousness occurs.
- Severe confusion develops.
- The person cannot drink fluids.
- Severe dehydration is suspected in an infant or older adult.

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is ORS better than plain water?

ORS helps replace both fluids and essential salts lost during dehydration.

### Can dehydration become dangerous?

Yes. Severe dehydration can be life-threatening without prompt treatment.
