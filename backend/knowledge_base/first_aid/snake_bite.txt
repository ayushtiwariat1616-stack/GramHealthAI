# Topic: Snake Bite

ID: FA002

Category: First Aid

---

## Overview

Snake bites can be dangerous, especially if caused by a venomous snake. Immediate first aid and rapid transport to a hospital are essential.

Do not try to identify the snake if it puts you at risk.

---

## Symptoms

Possible symptoms include:

- Bite marks
- Pain
- Swelling
- Redness
- Numbness
- Weakness
- Difficulty breathing
- Drooping eyelids
- Bleeding
- Vomiting

Symptoms vary depending on the type of snake.

---

## First Aid

If someone is bitten:

1. Stay calm and reassure the person.
2. Move away from the snake safely.
3. Keep the affected limb still.
4. Remove rings or tight clothing.
5. Keep the bite below heart level if possible.
6. Transport the person to the nearest hospital immediately.

---

## Do NOT

Do NOT:

- Cut the wound.
- Suck the venom.
- Apply ice.
- Apply chemicals or herbs.
- Use a tight tourniquet.
- Try to catch the snake.

---

## Hospital Treatment

Doctors may provide:

- Observation
- Antivenom (if needed)
- Pain relief
- Wound care
- Breathing support

---

## Emergency Signs

Call emergency medical services immediately if there is:

- Difficulty breathing
- Severe swelling
- Loss of consciousness
- Bleeding
- Seizures
- Weakness spreading through the body

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I wash the bite?

If possible, avoid excessive washing before medical assessment, as venom traces may sometimes help identify the type of snake. Follow local medical advice.

### Can all snake bites inject venom?

No. Some bites are "dry bites" with little or no venom, but every snake bite should be treated as a medical emergency.

---

## Disclaimer

This information is intended for first-aid education only and should not replace emergency medical care.
