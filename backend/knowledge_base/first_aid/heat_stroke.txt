# Topic: Heat Stroke

ID: FA006

Category: First Aid

---

## Overview

Heat stroke is a medical emergency that occurs when the body's temperature rises to dangerous levels and it can no longer cool itself effectively. Without prompt treatment, heat stroke can damage the brain, heart, kidneys, and other organs.

Heat stroke requires immediate emergency medical care.

---

## Risk Factors

Heat stroke is more likely during:

- Extremely hot weather
- Heavy physical activity
- Poor hydration
- Wearing heavy clothing
- Poor ventilation

Higher-risk groups include:

- Older adults
- Infants and young children
- People with chronic illnesses
- Outdoor workers

---

## Symptoms

Common symptoms include:

- Very high body temperature
- Hot, red skin
- Fast heartbeat
- Severe headache
- Dizziness
- Confusion
- Nausea or vomiting
- Difficulty walking
- Seizures
- Loss of consciousness

---

## First Aid

If heat stroke is suspected:

1. Call emergency medical services immediately.
2. Move the person to a cool, shaded place.
3. Remove excess clothing.
4. Cool the body using cool water, wet cloths, or fans.
5. Apply cool cloths to the neck, armpits, and groin.
6. Stay with the person until help arrives.

---

## If the Person Is Conscious

If fully awake and able to swallow safely:

- Offer small sips of cool water.

Do not force fluids if the person is confused or unconscious.

---

## Prevention

Reduce the risk by:

- Drinking enough water.
- Wearing light-colored clothing.
- Avoiding outdoor work during peak heat.
- Taking frequent breaks in the shade.
- Never leaving children or pets inside parked vehicles.

---

## Do NOT

Do NOT:

- Give alcohol.
- Give caffeine.
- Force water into an unconscious person.
- Delay calling for medical help.

---

## Emergency Signs

Heat stroke itself is an emergency.

Call emergency medical services immediately if the person has:

- Loss of consciousness
- Seizures
- Confusion
- Very high body temperature
- Difficulty breathing

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is heat stroke the same as heat exhaustion?

No. Heat stroke is much more serious and requires immediate medical treatment.

### Can heat stroke be prevented?

Yes. Staying hydrated, avoiding excessive heat exposure, and taking regular breaks greatly reduce the risk.

---

## Disclaimer

This information is intended for first-aid education only and should not replace emergency medical care.
