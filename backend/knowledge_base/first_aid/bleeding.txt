# Topic: Bleeding

ID: FA005

Category: First Aid

---

## Overview

Bleeding occurs when blood escapes from damaged blood vessels due to injury or illness. Severe bleeding can quickly become life-threatening if not controlled. Immediate first aid can reduce blood loss until medical help arrives.

---

## Types of Bleeding

Bleeding may be:

- Minor cuts and scrapes
- Deep wounds
- Nosebleeds
- Internal bleeding (not always visible)
- Severe external bleeding

---

## Signs of Severe Bleeding

Watch for:

- Continuous heavy bleeding
- Blood soaking through bandages
- Large pools of blood
- Pale or cold skin
- Weakness
- Dizziness
- Rapid heartbeat
- Confusion

---

## First Aid

If someone is bleeding:

1. Ensure the area is safe.
2. Wash your hands or wear gloves if available.
3. Apply firm direct pressure over the wound using a clean cloth or sterile dressing.
4. Keep pressure until the bleeding stops.
5. If the dressing becomes soaked, place another dressing on top without removing the first one.
6. Elevate the injured limb if possible and if no fracture is suspected.
7. Seek medical care if bleeding does not stop.

---

## Nosebleed First Aid

If the nose is bleeding:

1. Sit upright.
2. Lean slightly forward.
3. Pinch the soft part of the nose for 10–15 minutes.
4. Breathe through the mouth.
5. Avoid lying down.

---

## Do NOT

Do NOT:

- Remove dressings repeatedly.
- Apply mud, ash, or powders.
- Blow the nose during a nosebleed.
- Use a tourniquet unless trained or instructed by emergency services.

---

## When to Visit a Doctor

Seek medical care if:

- Bleeding continues after 10–15 minutes of direct pressure.
- The wound is deep.
- The injury was caused by an animal bite.
- The wound appears infected.
- Foreign objects remain inside the wound.

---

## Emergency Signs

Call emergency medical services immediately if:

- Heavy bleeding cannot be controlled.
- Blood is spurting from the wound.
- The person becomes unconscious.
- Signs of shock develop.
- Internal bleeding is suspected.

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I remove the first bandage if it becomes soaked?

No. Leave it in place and add another dressing on top while maintaining pressure.

### Should I wash a deep wound immediately?

Minor wounds may be gently cleaned. Severe wounds should first be controlled by direct pressure before medical evaluation.

---

## Disclaimer

This information is intended for first-aid education only and does not replace emergency medical care.
