# Topic: Electric Shock

ID: FA009

Category: First Aid

---

## Overview

An electric shock occurs when electricity passes through the body. Injuries can range from mild burns to life-threatening heart or breathing problems.

Always ensure your own safety before helping someone who has received an electric shock.

---

## Symptoms

A person may have:

- Burns
- Pain
- Muscle spasms
- Numbness
- Difficulty breathing
- Irregular heartbeat
- Loss of consciousness
- Cardiac arrest

---

## First Aid

If someone receives an electric shock:

1. Turn off the electrical power source if it is safe.
2. Do not touch the person until the electricity is disconnected.
3. Use a dry wooden or plastic object to move the electrical source away if necessary and safe.
4. Call emergency medical services.
5. Check if the person is breathing.
6. If trained, begin CPR if the person is not breathing and emergency services have been called.
7. Cover burns with a clean, dry dressing.

---

## Do NOT

Do NOT:

- Touch the person while electricity is still flowing.
- Use metal objects.
- Pour water on electrical equipment.
- Ignore even a minor electric shock.

---

## When to Visit a Doctor

Medical evaluation is recommended after any significant electric shock, especially if there are burns, pain, numbness, or muscle weakness.

---

## Emergency Signs

Call emergency medical services immediately if:

- The person is unconscious.
- Breathing has stopped.
- Chest pain occurs.
- There are severe burns.
- Cardiac arrest is suspected.

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a small electric shock still be dangerous?

Yes. Even mild shocks can affect the heart or cause internal injuries.

### Should I touch the person immediately?

No. Ensure the power source is disconnected before touching the person.

---

## Disclaimer

This information is intended for first-aid education only and should not replace emergency medical care.
