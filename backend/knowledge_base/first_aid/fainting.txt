# Topic: Fainting

ID: FA007

Category: First Aid

---

## Overview

Fainting (syncope) is a temporary loss of consciousness caused by a brief reduction in blood flow to the brain. Most people recover quickly, but fainting can sometimes be a sign of a serious medical condition.

---

## Common Causes

Fainting may occur due to:

- Standing for a long time
- Dehydration
- Low blood sugar
- Extreme heat
- Emotional stress
- Sudden pain
- Certain medical conditions

---

## Warning Signs

A person may experience:

- Dizziness
- Weakness
- Blurred vision
- Sweating
- Pale skin
- Nausea
- Ringing in the ears

---

## First Aid

If someone faints:

1. Help the person lie flat on their back.
2. Raise the legs about 20–30 cm if no injury is suspected.
3. Loosen tight clothing.
4. Ensure fresh air is available.
5. Check if the person is breathing normally.
6. Stay with the person until they recover.

---

## If the Person Wakes Up

- Allow them to rest.
- Offer water if fully awake.
- Help them sit up slowly.
- Avoid sudden standing.

---

## Do NOT

Do NOT:

- Throw water on the person's face.
- Shake the person.
- Give food or drink while unconscious.
- Allow them to stand immediately.

---

## When to Visit a Doctor

Medical evaluation is recommended if:

- It is the person's first fainting episode.
- Fainting occurs repeatedly.
- Recovery is slow.
- The person has heart disease.
- The person was injured during the fall.

---

## Emergency Signs

Call emergency medical services immediately if:

- The person does not wake within one minute.
- Breathing is abnormal.
- Chest pain occurs.
- A seizure occurs.
- Serious injury is suspected.

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is fainting always serious?

No. Many episodes are harmless, but some may indicate an underlying medical problem.

### Should I give water immediately?

Only after the person is fully awake and able to swallow safely.
