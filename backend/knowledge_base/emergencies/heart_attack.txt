# Topic: Heart Attack

ID: EM001

Category: Emergency

---

## Overview

A heart attack (myocardial infarction) occurs when blood flow to part of the heart muscle is blocked. Without quick treatment, the heart muscle can become permanently damaged.

A heart attack is a medical emergency. Immediate treatment can save a life.

---

## Common Symptoms

Symptoms may include:

- Severe chest pain or pressure
- Pain spreading to the left arm, shoulder, neck, jaw, or back
- Shortness of breath
- Cold sweating
- Nausea or vomiting
- Dizziness
- Extreme weakness

Some people, especially women, older adults, and people with diabetes, may have less typical symptoms such as unusual fatigue or mild chest discomfort.

---

## What To Do

If a heart attack is suspected:

1. Call emergency medical services immediately.
2. Keep the person sitting comfortably.
3. Loosen tight clothing.
4. Keep the person calm.
5. Do not allow unnecessary walking.
6. If the person becomes unconscious and is not breathing normally, begin CPR if you are trained.

---

## Do NOT

Do NOT:

- Ignore chest pain.
- Delay calling emergency services.
- Allow the person to drive themselves to the hospital.
- Give food or drinks if the person is unconscious.

---

## Risk Factors

The risk is higher in people with:

- High blood pressure
- Diabetes
- Smoking
- High cholesterol
- Obesity
- Family history of heart disease
- Lack of physical activity

---

## Emergency Signs

Call emergency medical services immediately if there is:

- Chest pain lasting more than a few minutes
- Difficulty breathing
- Collapse
- Loss of consciousness
- Cardiac arrest

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a heart attack happen without severe chest pain?

Yes. Some people experience mild symptoms or unusual fatigue, especially women, older adults, and people with diabetes.

### Should I wait to see if the pain goes away?

No. Seek emergency medical care immediately.
