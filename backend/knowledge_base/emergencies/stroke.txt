# Topic: Stroke

ID: EM002

Category: Emergency

---

## Overview

A stroke occurs when blood flow to part of the brain is interrupted or when a blood vessel in the brain bursts. Brain cells begin to die within minutes, making immediate medical treatment essential.

A stroke is a medical emergency.

---

## Common Symptoms

Remember the word **FAST**:

**F – Face:** One side of the face droops.

**A – Arms:** One arm becomes weak or numb.

**S – Speech:** Speech becomes slurred or difficult.

**T – Time:** Call emergency medical services immediately.

Other symptoms include:

- Sudden confusion
- Sudden severe headache
- Loss of balance
- Difficulty walking
- Vision problems
- Numbness on one side of the body

---

## What To Do

If stroke is suspected:

1. Call emergency medical services immediately.
2. Note the time symptoms started.
3. Keep the person comfortable.
4. Turn the person onto one side if vomiting occurs.
5. Monitor breathing until help arrives.

---

## Do NOT

Do NOT:

- Give food or water.
- Give medicines unless instructed by emergency professionals.
- Wait for symptoms to improve.

---

## Risk Factors

Risk increases with:

- High blood pressure
- Diabetes
- Smoking
- Heart disease
- High cholesterol
- Obesity

---

## Emergency Signs

Call emergency medical services immediately if FAST symptoms appear or if the person suddenly loses consciousness.

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can stroke symptoms disappear?

Sometimes symptoms improve temporarily, but urgent medical evaluation is still required.

### Why is quick treatment important?

Early treatment greatly improves the chance of recovery and reduces brain damage.
