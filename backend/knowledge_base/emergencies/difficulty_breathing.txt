# Topic: Difficulty Breathing

ID: EM003

Category: Emergency

---

## Overview

Difficulty breathing, also called shortness of breath, is a condition where a person has trouble getting enough air. It can occur suddenly or gradually and may be caused by asthma, severe allergic reactions, pneumonia, heart disease, or other serious medical conditions.

Severe difficulty breathing is a medical emergency.

---

## Common Symptoms

A person may experience:

- Shortness of breath
- Rapid breathing
- Chest tightness
- Wheezing
- Difficulty speaking full sentences
- Blue lips or fingertips
- Anxiety or restlessness
- Noisy breathing

---

## Common Causes

Difficulty breathing may occur due to:

- Asthma attack
- Severe allergic reaction (Anaphylaxis)
- Pneumonia
- COVID-19 or influenza
- Heart problems
- Choking
- Chronic lung diseases

---

## First Aid

If someone has difficulty breathing:

1. Call emergency medical services immediately if breathing is severe.
2. Help the person sit upright.
3. Loosen tight clothing.
4. Keep the person calm.
5. Encourage slow, steady breathing.
6. If the person uses a prescribed inhaler, help them use it according to medical advice.
7. Monitor breathing until medical help arrives.

---

## Do NOT

Do NOT:

- Force the person to lie flat.
- Give food or drinks if breathing is severely affected.
- Leave the person alone.
- Delay seeking emergency medical care.

---

## Emergency Signs

Call emergency medical services immediately if:

- The person cannot speak.
- Lips or face turn blue.
- Breathing becomes extremely fast or stops.
- Chest pain occurs.
- The person becomes unconscious.

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I let the person lie down?

No. Sitting upright usually makes breathing easier unless medical professionals advise otherwise.

### Can asthma cause difficulty breathing?

Yes. Asthma attacks commonly cause wheezing and shortness of breath.
