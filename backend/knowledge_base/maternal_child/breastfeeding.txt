# Topic: Breastfeeding

ID: MCH003

Category: Maternal & Child Health

---

## Overview

Breastfeeding is the best source of nutrition for most infants. Breast milk provides all the nutrients a baby needs during the first six months of life and also contains antibodies that help protect against infections.

The World Health Organization recommends exclusive breastfeeding for the first six months.

---

## Benefits for the Baby

Breastfeeding helps:

- Provide complete nutrition.
- Strengthen the immune system.
- Reduce the risk of diarrhea.
- Reduce respiratory infections.
- Support healthy brain development.
- Promote healthy growth.

---

## Benefits for the Mother

Breastfeeding may:

- Help the uterus return to its normal size.
- Reduce bleeding after childbirth.
- Strengthen bonding with the baby.
- Lower the risk of certain cancers.
- Support postpartum recovery.

---

## Exclusive Breastfeeding

For the first six months:

- Give only breast milk.
- Do not give water.
- Do not give honey.
- Do not give animal milk.
- Do not give solid foods unless advised by a healthcare professional.

---

## Feeding Frequency

Newborns usually feed:

- 8–12 times in 24 hours.
- Whenever they show signs of hunger.

Signs of hunger include:

- Sucking movements.
- Bringing hands to the mouth.
- Rooting (turning toward the breast).

---

## Good Breastfeeding Practices

- Hold the baby comfortably.
- Ensure a proper latch.
- Feed from both breasts.
- Burp the baby after feeding if needed.
- Continue breastfeeding during minor illnesses unless advised otherwise.

---

## When to Seek Medical Advice

Consult a healthcare professional if:

- The baby is not feeding well.
- Breastfeeding is painful.
- The baby is not gaining weight.
- The mother has fever or severe breast pain.

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should babies drink water before six months?

No. Healthy babies who are exclusively breastfed usually do not need additional water before six months.

### Can I continue breastfeeding if my baby has a cold?

In most cases, yes. Continue breastfeeding unless advised otherwise by a healthcare professional.
