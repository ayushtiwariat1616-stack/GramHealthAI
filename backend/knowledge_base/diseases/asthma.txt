# Topic: Asthma

ID: DIS014

Category: Chronic Respiratory Disease

---

## Overview

Asthma is a long-term disease that causes inflammation and narrowing of the airways, making breathing difficult. Symptoms may come and go and can be triggered by allergens, infections, exercise, smoke, or weather changes.

With proper treatment, most people with asthma can lead normal lives.

---

## Symptoms

Common symptoms include:

- Wheezing
- Shortness of breath
- Chest tightness
- Persistent cough
- Difficulty breathing, especially at night or early morning

Symptoms may worsen during exercise or respiratory infections.

---

## Causes

Asthma develops due to inflammation of the airways.

Common triggers include:

- Dust
- Smoke
- Pollen
- Pet dander
- Cold air
- Exercise
- Respiratory infections
- Air pollution

---

## Prevention

Help reduce asthma attacks by:

- Avoiding known triggers.
- Taking prescribed medicines regularly.
- Keeping indoor air clean.
- Avoiding tobacco smoke.
- Following your asthma action plan.

---

## Diagnosis

Doctors may recommend:

- Physical examination
- Spirometry (lung function test)
- Peak flow measurement
- Allergy testing (when needed)

---

## Treatment

Treatment may include:

- Inhalers
- Controller medicines
- Reliever medicines
- Avoiding triggers
- Regular follow-up

Do not stop asthma medicines without medical advice.

---

## When to Visit a Doctor

Consult a healthcare professional if:

- Symptoms occur frequently.
- Night-time coughing increases.
- Rescue inhaler is needed more often.
- Breathing becomes difficult.

---

## Emergency Signs

Seek immediate medical attention if:

- Severe difficulty breathing
- Lips or face become blue
- Unable to speak full sentences
- No relief after using a rescue inhaler
- Loss of consciousness

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can asthma be cured?

There is no permanent cure, but it can be controlled effectively with treatment.

### Is exercise safe for people with asthma?

Yes. Most people with well-controlled asthma can exercise safely after consulting their healthcare professional.
