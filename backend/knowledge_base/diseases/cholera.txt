# Topic: Cholera

ID: DIS004

Category: Infectious Disease

---

## Overview

Cholera is an acute diarrheal disease caused by the bacterium *Vibrio cholerae*. It spreads through contaminated food and water. Without prompt treatment, severe dehydration can become life-threatening, but most people recover completely with early medical care.

---

## Symptoms

Common symptoms include:

- Sudden watery diarrhea
- Vomiting
- Leg cramps
- Rapid dehydration
- Dry mouth
- Excessive thirst
- Weakness
- Low blood pressure

Symptoms may begin within a few hours to five days after infection.

---

## Causes

Cholera is caused by *Vibrio cholerae* bacteria.

The infection spreads through:

- Drinking contaminated water.
- Eating contaminated food.
- Poor sanitation.
- Poor hygiene.

It does NOT spread through casual contact or coughing.

---

## Risk Factors

Higher risk is seen in people who:

- Drink untreated water.
- Live in overcrowded areas.
- Have poor sanitation facilities.
- Eat contaminated food.

---

## Prevention

Reduce the risk by:

- Drinking boiled or treated water.
- Washing hands with soap.
- Eating properly cooked food.
- Washing fruits and vegetables.
- Using clean toilets.
- Avoiding contaminated water during floods.

---

## Diagnosis

Doctors may perform:

- Stool examination
- Stool culture
- Physical examination for dehydration

---

## Treatment

Treatment includes:

- Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS).
- Drinking plenty of safe fluids.
- Intravenous fluids for severe dehydration.
- Antibiotics only if prescribed by a healthcare professional.

Early rehydration saves lives.

---

## When to Visit a Doctor

Consult a healthcare professional immediately if:

- Diarrhea is severe.
- Vomiting prevents drinking fluids.
- Signs of dehydration appear.
- Symptoms worsen rapidly.

---

## Emergency Signs

Seek emergency medical care if there is:

- Extreme dehydration
- Very little urine output
- Sunken eyes
- Confusion
- Loss of consciousness
- Shock

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is cholera curable?

Yes. Most people recover completely with prompt treatment and rehydration.

### What is the biggest danger of cholera?

Severe dehydration.
