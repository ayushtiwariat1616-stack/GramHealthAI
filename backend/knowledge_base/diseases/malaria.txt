# Topic: Malaria

ID: DIS001

Category: Infectious Disease

---

## Overview

Malaria is a mosquito-borne infectious disease caused by Plasmodium parasites. It spreads through the bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito. Malaria remains a significant public health concern in many tropical and subtropical regions, including several parts of India. Early diagnosis and timely treatment are essential to prevent severe illness and complications.

---

## Symptoms

Common symptoms include:

- High fever
- Chills and shivering
- Sweating
- Headache
- Muscle pain
- Joint pain
- Fatigue or weakness
- Nausea
- Vomiting

Symptoms usually develop between 7 and 30 days after an infected mosquito bite.

---

## Causes

Malaria is caused by Plasmodium parasites, mainly:

- Plasmodium falciparum
- Plasmodium vivax
- Plasmodium malariae
- Plasmodium ovale
- Plasmodium knowlesi

The disease spreads through:

- Bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito.

Rarely, malaria may spread through:

- Blood transfusion with infected blood.
- Organ transplantation.
- Shared contaminated needles.
- From mother to baby during pregnancy or childbirth.

Malaria does NOT spread through:

- Touching an infected person.
- Sharing food or water.
- Coughing or sneezing.

---

## Prevention

You can reduce the risk of malaria by:

- Sleeping under insecticide-treated mosquito nets.
- Using mosquito repellents.
- Wearing full-sleeved clothing, especially during evening and night.
- Installing window and door screens.
- Eliminating stagnant water around homes.
- Keeping water storage containers covered.
- Seeking preventive medicines before travelling to malaria-endemic regions if advised by a doctor.

---

## Treatment

If malaria is suspected:

- Visit a healthcare professional immediately.
- Confirm the diagnosis through a blood test.
- Take only medicines prescribed by a qualified doctor.
- Complete the full prescribed treatment course.
- Drink adequate fluids.
- Get sufficient rest.

Do not self-medicate.

---

## Emergency Signs

Seek immediate medical care if the person develops:

- Difficulty breathing
- Continuous vomiting
- Severe weakness
- Confusion
- Seizures
- Loss of consciousness
- Yellowing of eyes or skin
- Very high fever that does not improve

---

## Disclaimer

This information is intended for health education only and does not replace professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Always consult a qualified healthcare professional if malaria is suspected.
