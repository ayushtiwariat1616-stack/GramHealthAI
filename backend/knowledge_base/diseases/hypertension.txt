# Topic: Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)

ID: DIS012

Category: Non-Communicable Disease

---

## Overview

Hypertension, also known as high blood pressure, is a condition in which the force of blood against the artery walls remains consistently high. If left untreated, it increases the risk of heart disease, stroke, kidney disease, and other serious health problems.

Many people with hypertension have no symptoms.

---

## Symptoms

Most people have no symptoms.

When blood pressure becomes very high, symptoms may include:

- Severe headache
- Dizziness
- Blurred vision
- Chest pain
- Shortness of breath
- Nosebleeds (occasionally)

---

## Causes

Hypertension may be caused by:

- Family history
- High salt intake
- Obesity
- Lack of physical activity
- Smoking
- Excessive alcohol consumption
- Stress
- Kidney disease

---

## Prevention

Reduce the risk by:

- Eating less salt
- Exercising regularly
- Maintaining a healthy weight
- Avoiding tobacco
- Limiting alcohol
- Managing stress
- Regular blood pressure check-ups

---

## Diagnosis

Doctors usually diagnose hypertension by measuring blood pressure on multiple occasions.

Additional tests may include:

- Blood tests
- Urine tests
- ECG
- Kidney function tests

---

## Treatment

Treatment may include:

- Lifestyle modifications
- Blood pressure medicines
- Regular monitoring
- Healthy diet
- Physical activity

Do not stop medicines without medical advice.

---

## When to Visit a Doctor

Consult a healthcare professional if:

- Blood pressure remains consistently high.
- You experience headaches or dizziness.
- Chest pain develops.

---

## Emergency Signs

Seek emergency medical care immediately if there is:

- Severe chest pain
- Difficulty breathing
- Sudden weakness
- Difficulty speaking
- Vision loss
- Loss of consciousness

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can hypertension be prevented?

Many cases can be prevented through healthy lifestyle habits.

### Why is hypertension called the "silent killer"?

Because many people have no symptoms until serious complications develop.
