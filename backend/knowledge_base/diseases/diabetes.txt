# Topic: Diabetes

ID: DIS011

Category: Non-Communicable Disease

---

## Overview

Diabetes is a long-term condition in which the body cannot properly control blood sugar (glucose) levels. High blood sugar over time can damage the heart, kidneys, eyes, nerves, and blood vessels.

The most common types are Type 1 Diabetes, Type 2 Diabetes, and Gestational Diabetes.

---

## Symptoms

Common symptoms include:

- Frequent urination
- Increased thirst
- Increased hunger
- Unexplained weight loss
- Fatigue
- Blurred vision
- Slow wound healing
- Frequent infections
- Tingling or numbness in hands and feet

---

## Causes

Diabetes develops when the body cannot produce enough insulin or cannot use insulin effectively.

Risk factors include:

- Family history
- Being overweight or obese
- Physical inactivity
- Unhealthy diet
- Increasing age
- High blood pressure

---

## Prevention

The risk of Type 2 Diabetes can be reduced by:

- Maintaining a healthy weight
- Exercising regularly
- Eating a balanced diet
- Limiting sugary foods and drinks
- Avoiding tobacco
- Regular health check-ups

---

## Diagnosis

Doctors may recommend:

- Fasting Blood Sugar (FBS)
- Random Blood Sugar (RBS)
- HbA1c test
- Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT)

---

## Treatment

Treatment may include:

- Healthy eating
- Regular physical activity
- Diabetes medicines
- Insulin therapy (when prescribed)
- Regular blood sugar monitoring

Never stop diabetes medicines without consulting a healthcare professional.

---

## When to Visit a Doctor

Consult a healthcare professional if:

- You have frequent urination and excessive thirst.
- Blood sugar remains high.
- Wounds heal slowly.
- Vision becomes blurry.

---

## Emergency Signs

Seek emergency medical care if there is:

- Severe confusion
- Difficulty breathing
- Loss of consciousness
- Persistent vomiting
- Very high or very low blood sugar symptoms

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can diabetes be cured?

There is currently no cure, but it can be managed effectively with lifestyle changes and treatment.

### Can eating sugar alone cause diabetes?

No. Diabetes develops due to multiple factors including genetics, body weight, and lifestyle.
