# Topic: Food Safety

ID: PRE003

Category: Prevention

---

## Overview

Food safety means handling, preparing, cooking, and storing food properly to prevent food-borne illnesses. Unsafe food may contain harmful bacteria, viruses, parasites, or chemicals that can cause diseases such as diarrhea, food poisoning, typhoid, and hepatitis A.

Following safe food practices protects the health of individuals and families.

---

## Safe Food Practices

Always:

- Wash hands before preparing food.
- Wash fruits and vegetables with clean water.
- Cook food thoroughly.
- Drink safe, clean water.
- Cover cooked food.
- Refrigerate perishable foods promptly.
- Use clean utensils and cutting boards.

---

## Foods That Need Extra Care

Pay special attention to:

- Meat
- Fish
- Eggs
- Milk and dairy products
- Leftover food
- Street food that is not freshly prepared

---

## Prevent Cross-Contamination

- Keep raw and cooked foods separate.
- Use different knives and cutting boards for raw meat and vegetables.
- Wash utensils after handling raw meat.

---

## Safe Food Storage

- Keep hot food hot and cold food cold.
- Store leftovers in clean, covered containers.
- Refrigerate cooked food within two hours.
- Check expiry dates before consuming packaged foods.

---

## Signs Food May Be Unsafe

Do not eat food if it has:

- Bad smell
- Mold
- Unusual color
- Swollen packaging
- Expired date
- Sour taste (when not expected)

---

## Diseases Prevented

Safe food practices help prevent:

- Food poisoning
- Diarrhea
- Typhoid
- Cholera
- Hepatitis A

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is reheating food enough to make it safe?

Not always. Food that has spoiled should be thrown away even if reheated.

### Should fruits and vegetables be washed?

Yes. Wash them thoroughly with clean water before eating or cooking.
